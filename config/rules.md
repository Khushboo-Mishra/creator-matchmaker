# Campaign rules

## Product

Milanote is a visual workspace for organizing creative projects.

It allows users to collect and organize notes, images, videos, links,
sketches, research, tasks, and other project information on visual boards.

Milanote supports creative workflows including design and illustration,
filmmaking, photography, writing, marketing, content creation, interior
design, fashion design, art, and game development.

Users can collect inspiration from the web, organize research and ideas,
use templates and to-do lists to plan projects, and access their work
across supported devices.

Boards can also be shared with other people for viewing, commenting,
feedback, or collaborative editing.

## Audience

Creative professionals, emerging creators, students, and teams whose work
involves collecting inspiration, researching ideas, visually planning
projects, organizing creative assets, or collaborating on creative work.

Relevant audiences include designers, filmmakers, photographers, writers,
illustrators, content creators, marketers, artists, students, and other
people managing visual, research-heavy, or idea-driven projects.

Creator relevance should be based not only on the creator's professional
label, but also on whether their content and audience demonstrate creative
planning, research, ideation, organization, or collaborative workflows.

## Message

Turn scattered inspiration into an organized creative project.

The campaign should show how different kinds of creators can use Milanote
to move from an early idea or inspiration toward an organized plan,
individually or collaboratively.

Creator content should demonstrate Milanote through a genuine creative
workflow rather than simply listing product features.

The creator should show where Milanote naturally fits into their existing
process, such as collecting inspiration, researching, moodboarding,
planning, organizing creative material, receiving feedback, or
collaborating with others.

## Allowed claims

Creators may state or demonstrate that Milanote:

- is a visual workspace for organizing creative projects;
- allows users to organize projects visually using boards;
- can combine notes, images, videos, links, sketches, research, and tasks;
- can be used to collect ideas, inspiration, and research;
- provides tools for project planning, including to-do lists;
- provides templates for different types of creative projects;
- allows users to collect inspiration from the web;
- allows boards to be shared with other people;
- supports commenting, feedback, and collaborative editing on shared boards;
- can be used across a range of creative workflows;
- can be accessed through supported desktop/web and mobile experiences;
- provides options for exporting work; and
- offers a free plan.

Creators may describe their own genuine experience using Milanote as long
as personal opinions and experiences are clearly presented as such.

## Banned claims

Creators must not:

- claim Milanote guarantees increased creativity, productivity, audience
  growth, income, or professional success;
- claim Milanote is objectively the best creative or productivity tool;
- make unsupported claims that Milanote is definitively better than
  competing products;
- invent or imply features Milanote does not offer;
- make unsupported claims about AI capabilities;
- make unsupported claims about privacy, security, performance, or
  reliability;
- claim that using Milanote guarantees a particular creative or
  professional outcome;
- present paid or sponsored endorsements as independent recommendations;
- describe experiences or results the creator has not genuinely had;
- make pricing, plan-limit, or promotional claims unless they reflect
  current campaign information; or
- make factual product claims unsupported by Milanote's published
  information.

## Disclosure format

Sponsored creator content must clearly disclose the creator's material
relationship with Milanote.

For YouTube video content, the disclosure should be easy to notice and
understand and should appear in the video itself, rather than only in the
video description.

Clear language such as "This video is sponsored by Milanote" or
"Sponsored by Milanote" should be used.

Creators should also use YouTube's applicable paid-promotion disclosure
tools. The sponsorship disclosure should not be hidden among hashtags,
links, or other text where viewers are unlikely to notice it.
