Casting a roster, not ranking a list

A shortlist ranks creators against each other. A roster gives them different jobs, and swapping one creator moves the others. Every candidate here was scored once across six dimensions and written to disk, so changing what the campaign values is arithmetic over stored numbers rather than a new round of API calls.

    Architecture

    Campaign brief in, defensible roster out

    The expensive steps run once per campaign. The step a strategist actually works in costs nothing.

    01 DISCOVER

    Brief becomes queries

    Gemini turns the campaign rules into YouTube search queries and returns candidates, each carrying the query and video that surfaced it.

    02 PULL

    Cache the evidence

    50 uploads, statistics and sampled comments per channel, resolved by handle at one quota unit and cached to disk. Never re-pulled during a demo.

    03 SCORE ONCE

    Six dimensions, persisted

    Three arithmetic, three from Gemini. Every score stored with a one-sentence reason and validated against a JSON schema.

    04 CAST

    Weight, then assign roles

    Sliders reorder instantly from stored scores. Chosen creators take distinct roles that redistribute when one is swapped out.

    The decision everything follows from

    Score every channel once and persist the numbers. A weight change is arithmetic over stored values, so no API call runs when a weight moves. Fifty weightings in a client meeting cost nothing, and the ranking that comes out can be reproduced exactly tomorrow.

    Why half the dimensions are formulas

    Engagement, momentum and speed to activate are arithmetic anyone can recheck in a spreadsheet. When a client asks where a number came from, half the answers need no trust in a model at all. Medians rather than means, so one viral video cannot rescue a dead channel.

    The six dimensions

    DimensionSourceMethod
    Audience relevanceGeminiReads sampled comments, not subscriber counts
    EngagementArithmetic(likes + comments) / views across the last 15 uploads
    Brand fitGeminiReads recent titles against the campaign rules file
    MomentumArithmeticMedian views of the last 5 uploads over the median of the previous 10
    Speed to activateArithmeticUploads published in the last 90 days
    Trend fitGeminiRecent subjects against a dated, cited trend snapshot

    Provenance

    What is real on this page, and what is not

    A tool a strategist has to defend in a client meeting is only useful if its limits are written down before the client finds them.

    On this pageStatus
    Channel names, subscriber counts, upload statisticsReal, pulled from the YouTube Data API
    Engagement, momentum, speed to activateReal, computed for all 70
    Audience relevance, brand fit, trend fitReal Gemini output, but only for the channels not tagged partial
    Preset weightings and the four role profilesPrototype defaults chosen by us, not agreed campaign settings
    Trend snapshot behind trend fitResearched and cited, not live Search-grounded

    Not better picks

    There is no ground truth on which past creator selections succeeded, so accuracy cannot be demonstrated. The claim is faster and explainable, which is why every dimension carries a reason.

    No audience demographics

    Age and location are owner-only through YouTube Analytics. Audience relevance is a comment-based proxy and is labelled as one.

    Engagement favours mid-size creators

    Engagement rate falls as a channel grows, so the metric structurally favours mid-size channels. A deliberate bias, documented rather than hidden.
